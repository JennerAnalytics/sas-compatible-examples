# When should Americans buy their Thanksgiving turkey?

**Short answer: now. The best advertised prices come about two weeks before Thanksgiving, not in the final days.**

We analyzed 22 seasons of USDA records of advertised supermarket prices for frozen whole turkeys (2005-2026), and the pattern repeats most years:

- In mid-October few stores advertise turkeys, and the ones that do charge about 11% more than the season's low.
- Prices fall through early November as more stores join in, and bottom out in **mid-November**, about two weeks before the holiday.
- In **Thanksgiving week** the advertised price is higher again, about 11% above the low by Thanksgiving Day.

## The 2026 forecast

- The latest USDA report (ad week 31 October - 6 November) puts the national average advertised price at **$1.19 per lb**, down from $1.26 a week earlier.
- Our model expects the cheapest day to be around **Saturday 14 November, at about $1.16 per lb** (95% prediction interval $0.94-$1.43).
- By **Wednesday 25 November** the expected advertised price rises to about **$1.27 per lb** ($1.02-$1.57).
- On a 16-lb bird, waiting until the day before Thanksgiving costs about **$1.72** on average.
- Across 20,000 simulated price paths, this week's price beat Thanksgiving week's **79%** of the time.

## How sure are we?

- Differences between nearby days are small compared with the uncertainty. The useful message is "buy in the next week or so rather than at the last minute", not that one particular day is the cheapest.
- These are **national average advertised supermarket prices** for conventional frozen whole turkeys. Loyalty-card prices and "free turkey with a $100 shop" offers are not in the data, and your store may differ.
- Day-to-day prices aren't published (ads run for a week), so the daily curve is modelled.
- Turkeys are dearer this year: the advertised price in early November was $1.19 per lb, against $0.93 a year earlier.

## What else the data show

- **The turkey is a loss leader.** In 2022 and 2025 supermarkets advertised frozen turkeys about 44% below the wholesale price. Wholesale prices moved between $0.96 and $1.75 per lb over four years. The advertised price stayed between $0.95 and $1.11.
- **More ads, lower prices.** Weeks when more stores advertise turkeys have lower advertised prices.
- **Fresh costs more.** In the two ad weeks through Thanksgiving, fresh whole turkeys are advertised about 75% above frozen on average, and about 90% above in recent years.
- **Regions differ.** The Southeast and Midwest advertise the lowest prices, and the Northwest the highest.
